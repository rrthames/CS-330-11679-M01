# CS-330-11679-M01

Summarized Reflection
Working on this 3D scene project strengthened how I approach both design and development while clarifying the value of computational graphics for my future path.
I learned to treat design as an iterative, deliberate process rather than a one-time creative step. New skills included planning spatial relationships, lighting, materials, and camera movement with purpose, plus documenting decisions so the rationale stays clear. I followed a cycle of concept sketching, blocking out the scene, refining aesthetics and interaction, and reviewing against goals. These habits, early prototyping, intentional documentation, and evaluating trade-offs, transfer directly to future projects where clarity and maintainability matter.

I adopted incremental development: start with a basic geometry and camera setup, then layer materials, lighting, animation, and interaction. Iteration was central, each milestone revealed issues in performance, visual coherence, or usability that I fixed before moving forward. Across the milestones my process became more structured: I planned smaller testable pieces, versioned changes carefully, and used debugging and profiling earlier. This evolution produced a more complete, stable final scene and a clearer development workflow I can reuse.

Computational graphics and visualization gave me practical experience translating abstract ideas into interactive 3D environments, strengthening spatial reasoning, problem decomposition, and the ability to communicate complex systems visually. These skills support further study in areas that rely on simulation, data visualization, or interactive media, and they are directly useful in professional roles involving 3D content, technical art, simulation, or any field that benefits from clear visual representation of complex information.
Together, the 3D scene and Design Decisions document show both the finished work and the deliberate process behind it.
